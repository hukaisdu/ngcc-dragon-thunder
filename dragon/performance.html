† The SHA3-512 and SM3 baselines are portable software implementations that do not use the Armv8.2 SHA-3 / SM3 instruction extensions (spec Table 15 footnotes). All three Dragon instances stay below the SHA3-512 baseline at every tested length on both platforms, and Dragon-512 / Dragon-768 also stay below SM3 everywhere; Dragon-1024 crosses above SM3 from S4 on (x86-64 10.44 vs 9.28 cpb), with a long-message cost close to SM3 (spec §5.1 Observations and §6). The measured optimized x86 implementation costs ≈38.8 cycles per permutation round (design estimate ≈40, spec p. 33). The XOF instances are not covered by the software self-assessment.

At the submitted synthesis results the three Dragon instances close timing only at 251–258 MHz (critical path 3.88–3.98 ns, bounded by the ARX carry chains), so their throughput stays below the SHA3-512 baseline synthesized at 1190 MHz; cycle counts are lower at 1 KiB / 64 KiB (up to 1.8×) and power is roughly half (spec p. 35).

Independent reference-implementation check (ngcc.dev)

Separately from the submission package, ngcc.dev measured the reference implementation (unoptimized placeholder-quality code) on its own x86_1 system. These numbers characterize the reference code, not the design’s optimized speed, and are not comparable with the self-assessment tables above; they are kept here as an independent correctness-and-timing cross-check.
