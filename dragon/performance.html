Performancedesigner self-assessment from the submission package

One-line takeaways — Software: all three fixed-output instances beat SHA3-512 at every tested length on both platforms (Dragon-512 long messages 4.87 vs 12.46 cpb, ≈ 2.6×; ARM 4.47 vs 15.49, ≈ 3.5×); Dragon-512/768 also stay below SM3 throughout. Hardware: the first synthesis (251–258 MHz) trails SHA3-512 in throughput, though cycle counts are up to 1.8× lower and power roughly halves.
How to read: cpb = cycles per byte, lower is faster; S1–S8 = message lengths 32 B → 64 KiB.

Design-team self-assessment (submission package, spec §5.1)

† The SHA3-512 and SM3 baselines are portable software implementations that do not use the Armv8.2 SHA-3 / SM3 instruction extensions (spec Table 15 footnotes). All three Dragon instances stay below the SHA3-512 baseline at every tested length on both platforms, and Dragon-512 / Dragon-768 also stay below SM3 everywhere; Dragon-1024 crosses above SM3 from S4 on (x86-64 10.44 vs 9.28 cpb), with a long-message cost close to SM3 (spec §5.1 Observations and §6). The measured optimized x86 implementation costs ≈38.8 cycles per permutation round (design estimate ≈40, spec p. 33). The XOF instances are not covered by the software self-assessment.

Round-based architecture (one round per clock cycle), VHDL + ModelSim, Synopsys Design Compiler. Values verified against the LaTeX source. At the submitted synthesis results the three Dragon instances close timing only at 251–258 MHz (critical path 3.88–3.98 ns, bounded by the ARX carry chains), so their throughput stays below the SHA3-512 baseline synthesized at 1190 MHz; cycle counts are lower at 1 KiB / 64 KiB (up to 1.8×) and power is roughly half (spec p. 35).
