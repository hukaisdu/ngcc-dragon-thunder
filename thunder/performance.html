† The SHA3-512 and SM3 baselines are portable software implementations that do not use the Armv8.2 SHA-3 / SM3 instruction extensions (spec Table 19 footnotes). Per the spec: Thunder-512 and Thunder-768 stay below SHA3-512 at every tested length on both platforms; Thunder-1024 is faster than SHA3-512 up to S3 on x86-64 and at all ARM lengths except S2 (spec §5.1). The design-level estimate is ≈75 cycles per round and the measured x86 implementation costs ≈74, consistent with the port-throughput analysis; the ARM implementation is slightly slower due to register pressure and stack spilling (spec p. 41). The XOF instances are not covered by the software self-assessment.

Round-based architecture (one round per clock cycle), VHDL + ModelSim, Synopsys Design Compiler. Values verified against the LaTeX source. Per the spec: Thunder-512 cuts the SHA3-512 cycle count by 32% / 49% / 50% (32 B / 1 KiB / 64 KiB), giving throughput gains of 32% / 77% / 82% despite a slightly longer critical path (0.93 vs 0.84 ns); its throughput-to-area ratio is lower only at 32 B and overtakes SHA3-512 by 13% / 16% at 1 KiB / 64 KiB. Thunder-768 and Thunder-1024 reach higher throughput than SHA3-512 at all three lengths (+51% / +33% at 64 KiB) (spec p. 44).

Independent reference-implementation check (ngcc.dev)

Separately from the submission package, ngcc.dev measured the reference implementation (unoptimized placeholder-quality code) on its own x86_1 system. These numbers characterize the reference code, not the design’s optimized speed, and are not comparable with the self-assessment tables above; they are kept here as an independent correctness-and-timing cross-check.
